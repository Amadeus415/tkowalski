Functional Capacity Evaluations

Six FCE formats, from a 90-minute return-to-work check to a full 8-hour evaluation.

Consistency, reliability of effort, pain magnification, pain migration and sub-maximal effort are reviewed and compared across the evaluation.

8hours

Full FCE

When reliability of effort is in question.

  • Completed as one full day or two 4-hour sessions.
  • Used when the treating physician, adjuster or case manager questions reliability of effort regarding return to work (RTW), RTW at full capacity, with restrictions or modifications, or a potential Permanent and Stationary status.
  • Reviews consistency, reliability, pain magnification, pain migration and sub-maximal effort.
6hours

Condensed FCE

Sedentary positions, re-checks and transfers.

  • The same reliability-of-effort parameters as the 8-hour FCE, condensed.
  • Suited to sedentary rather than moderate-to-heavy positions: an office worker rather than a construction worker, a computer operator rather than a Muni driver.
  • Also used for an FCE re-check, RTW to new position requirements, or a transfer within a comparable department.
4hours

Focused FCE

Specific RTW or therapy questions.

  • A one-day evaluation for specific RTW issues, return to therapy, or work hardening.
  • Once reliability of effort is resolved, reviews lift, carry, push, pull and other position-related activities.
  • Assumes a consistent, reliable presentation; provides the parameters needed to keep the case moving.
2hours

Pre-employment FCE

Before a position offer.

  • Reviews the individual's ability to perform to the requirements of the position.
  • For individuals without an employment-related injury relevant to the upcoming position.
4+hours

On-site FCE

When the worksite matters.

  • In selected situations, completed at the individual's work location, per the demands of the position.
  • A minimum of 4 hours is suggested.
90minutes

Brief Return-to-Work

Rapid RTW, same-day report.

  • When all parties want a rapid return to work.
  • Coordinated to job specifics, with a same-day report.

Please include a current position description. If one isn't available, a job analysis should be completed before the FCE.

Ergonomic Evaluations

A one-on-one look at the workstation behind the pain.

An evaluation of a worker's workplace to prevent, reduce or eliminate pain and discomfort that has been identified as workstation related. The current supervisor is invited to attend, and post-evaluation training is provided.

  • On site. Performed at the employer's location.
  • Employee present. Every evaluation is done with the employee.
  • Pre-approved rate. Billed at a rate approved before the evaluation.
  • 2 business days. Reports completed and forwarded.
Job Analysis

An objective record of what the position actually demands.

A one-on-one evaluation of the position's requirements as they relate to core and secondary activities. The report is completed and forwarded within 2 business days.

  • Based on the job description. Every analysis starts from a current description.
  • Position, not person. Reports are generic to the position rather than the individual.
  • Supervisor reviewed. Each report is reviewed with the supervisor.
  • Confidential. Handled in line with HIPAA.
